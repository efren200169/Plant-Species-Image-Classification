# Plant-Species-Image-Classification

## A. Project Overview

This project is about legumes, which are plants that produce pods with seeds inside, such as beans, peas, and peanuts. The purpose of the project is to study the different types of legumes, their importance as a source of food, and their benefits to human health and the environment. Legumes are rich in protein, vitamins, and minerals, and they also help improve soil fertility by adding nitrogen to the soil. This project may include planting legumes, observing their growth, and learning how they contribute to sustainable agriculture and nutrition.

## Brief Description of the Project:

The purpose of the image classification model is to automatically identify and classify different types of legumes based on their images. This model helps recognize legumes such as beans, peas, and peanuts quickly and accurately. It can also help students and researchers organize data, reduce manual work, and improve the accuracy of identifying legumes.

## Reflection Questions
1. How did the number of images per class affect your model’s accuracy?
- The model’s accuracy improves when there are more images per class because the model learns better. Fewer images per class can reduce accuracy and cause poor predictions.
2. Which plant species were most commonly misclassified and why?
- Lentils and mung beans were most commonly misclassified because they look very similar in shape, size, and color, which made it difficult for the model to distinguish between them.
3. How did changing the epochs, batch size, or learning rate affect the training results?
- Increasing the epochs improved accuracy but too many caused overfitting. Smaller batch sizes gave better accuracy but slower training. Lower learning rates made training slower but more stable and accurate, while higher learning rates made training faster but less accurate.
4. What challenges did you encounter during dataset collection and labeling?
- The main challenges were limited images, similar appearance of plant species, poor image quality, risk of incorrect labeling, and the time-consuming process of collecting and labeling the dataset.
5. If you were to improve your model, what specific changes would you make and why?
- To improve the model, I would add more and balanced images, improve image quality, use data augmentation, and adjust the training parameters. These changes would help increase accuracy and make the model more reliable.
